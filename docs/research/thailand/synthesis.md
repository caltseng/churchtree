# Original synthesis

Citation IDs resolve to the [passage index](sources.md). A country is an
organizing geography, not a denomination or an uninterrupted Christian tree.

## Ports, resident missions and shifting jurisdictions

Secure resident Catholic mission evidence begins here with Dominicans in
**1567**, not with a claim that no Christian had previously reached Siam.
The Bangkok church historian explicitly distinguishes hypothetical Portuguese
chaplains and an unconfirmed 1544 royal conversion from resident mission.
Francis Xavier's interest is not a visit. Portuguese patronage, later French
apostolic vicars, and Portuguese, Japanese and Vietnamese Christians cannot
be collapsed into one French national import. Lambert's arrival in 1662,
the 1664 synod and 1669 vicariate are different events; seminary and hospital
projects were institutions of formation and service, not new denominations.
See [C01-C03](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index).

Narai's diplomacy and patronage did not mean his conversion. Strathern argues
that Buddhist intellectuals, monastic networks and popular mobilization matter
alongside court rivalry, French troops and imperial ambitions in 1688.
Christian imprisonment was real, but motives and phases differed: anti-conversion
mobilization and later hostage bargaining are not the same explanation.
Neither a pure "religious hatred" story nor a pure "secular coup" explains
every episode. Ayutthaya's 1767 destruction and subsequent expulsions/returns
again disrupted communities without proving universal extinction.
[C04-C05](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

The refugee movement reported in 1835 is part of Catholic history, not a
footnote to European clergy. Historical "Siam" vicariates included territories
now in other states: the 1841 eastern/western division must not be mapped
onto present national borders. Later northern "Laos Mission" similarly names
a Lanna/northern Tai field, not today's Lao state. Modern Thailand is the
selection boundary, not a timeless ethnic/state container.
[C07-C08](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

## Local agency, language, work and unequal power

Protestant mission is conventionally dated from 1828, long after Catholic
presence. In 1835 Emelie Royce Bradley's family biography places her language,
dispensary and domestic work alongside medical encounters with court and
poorer patients. This interested text reproduces disparaging assumptions
about Buddhists and class; those judgments are evidence of missionary
perspective, not this packet's judgment of Thai society.
[C06/C34](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Daniel **and Sophia** McGilvary arrived in Chiang Mai in 1867. Sophia's 1868
women's literacy teaching and the 1876 girls' class preceded the formal 1879
school. Swanson restores local women's work: Chantah, Buk, Kom Tip and
Nyawt Huan were teachers/workers, not merely passive recipients. Kom Tip
became head assistant in 1888 and subsequently taught in Lamphun. Salaried
teaching and literacy created possibilities while missionary domestic
discipline constrained them. Royal/state education and print also mattered;
no sole Christian invention of Thai women's emancipation is claimed.
[C08/C11](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Noi Sunya and Nan Chai's 1869 executions show the stakes for local converts,
not simply foreign missionaries. The 1878 edict in McGilvary's later English
translation addressed conscience, marriage and labor/Sabbath conflict in
northern jurisdictions. Its translated date is not a newly checked Thai
royal original, nor a guarantee that every locality enjoyed modern freedom.
[C09-C10](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Catholic schools, sisters' nursing and local religious formation also shaped
Christian institutional life: Assumption College 1885 and the St Paul de
Chartres sisters'1898 arrival are examples, not the whole education/medical
history. Later CCT social action included leprosy rehabilitation, urban and
rural development and refugee aid. Institutional self-reports do not measure
how all patients or pupils received religious authority.
[C12/C33](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Translation was multilingual and socially uneven. Northern Thai printing
began in 1892, the NT was completed in 1914, and the OT remained incomplete
when translation was suspended about 1920. Swanson's critique addresses
missionary control of scriptural expertise; he explicitly cannot prove a
simple causal path from fragmentary delivery to later Bible use. National
Thai edition milestones 1971 and 2011 are separate from northern Thai and
Kuy 1979. Thai translators/stylists and terminology negotiations are not
invisible, but their individual biographies remain underresearched. The
Society's conflicting early dates are held pending edition/imprint study.
[C13/C21/C24/C28/C31](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

## Union, war, diversification and minorities

Adventist permanent mission 1918-1919 must be distinguished from earlier
colportage and from Chinese migrant/lay networks, including the Tan family.
Its medical and school development reached regions outside the northern
Presbyterian story. The 1987 Haadyai hospital closure complicates any
uninterrupted-growth narrative; institutional attribution to staffing is
not independently verified. [C14/C26](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

The 1934 Church in Siam/CCT union involved Presbyterians and **some**
Baptist/Churches of Christ congregations, not every Protestant. John Sung's
1938/1939 visits and Boonmark Kittisarn's participation brought revival and
conflict, not a single new nationwide denomination. Japanese invasion in 1941
removed/interned foreign missionaries and increased Thai responsibility.
The Songkhon killings in 1940 involved a catechist/father, sisters and
laywomen. The Catholic biography links police pressure with the conflict
against French Indochina; that explanation remains attributed rather than
independently adjudicated. Later Catholic commemoration recognizes local leadership after
a priest's expulsion. Nicholas Bunkerd Nitbamrung's 1944 prison death is a
separate wartime history. No national casualty total or adjudication of
every accusation follows from these sources.
[C15-C17](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Presbyterian institutional transfer in 1957, Disciples transfer 1961 and
CCT formal self-government 1976 mark different stages, not an instantaneous
end to foreign money or senior influence. The Catholic hierarchy 1965 is
likewise a jurisdictional change, not the beginning of Catholicism or proof
that every institution had indigenous control.
[C15/C19/C22](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Karen 1955 and Lahu 1971 conventions later affiliated with CCT while retaining
identities. "Minority churches" does not name one ethnicity or theology.
Hayami's abstract treats Christian/Buddhist Karen diversity and changing
identity during state integration; it does not license a universal story
of conversion or an ancient Christian "lost book" ancestry. A partial CCT
series cited by Swanson rises from5% in one minority district in 1980 to38%
across four in 1997, but district additions/classification and missing
communities prevent an all-Thailand conversion curve.
[C18/C25; S03 xviii](sources.md)

EFT formalization 1969 offered cooperation and state representation beyond
CCT; membership in an umbrella is not merger into one church. Pentecostal/
charismatic and independent urban congregations, including Romklao 1979 and
Hope of Bangkok 1981, add other strategies, worship styles, age/class settings
and conflicts. These examples do not exhaust small churches or their splits.
[C20/C23](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

## Transborder connections and what numbers cannot tell

Cambodian refugee aid in the 1970s, subsequent Myanmar work and Asian
missionary links show movements across the selection boundary. Moscow's
1999 parish decision and 2019 transborder diocese introduce modern
Orthodox/diaspora connections without creating descent from the older
Catholic or Protestant missions. No contemporary vulnerable roster or
meeting-site map is published.
[C27/C33](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

The 2023 religious-freedom report quotes a **December 2021 administrative
1.2% Christian affiliation figure**. Its midyear 2023 population estimate is
not the matching denominator, and questionnaire/sampling methods are not
reproduced. EFT's 2018 age 15+ affiliated count and Hayami's 1986/1988
baptized Karen counts measure different populations/years. No addition,
interpolation, national growth rate, or 2026 estimate is defensible here.
The report's 2023 legal description distinguishes registration, recognized
groups, benefits and missionary quotas from constitutional Buddhist
institutions; it is not legal advice or proof of uniform local experience.
[C25/C29-C30/C32](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Regional comparison remains asymmetric: Bangkok/Lanna have substantially
more inspected evidence than Isan, the Malay-majority south or border
displacement histories. Theology, worship, retention, class mobility,
contemporary independent networks and Thai/minority-language testimony
need further research. These are documented gaps, not evidence that
Christians or consequential histories were absent.
