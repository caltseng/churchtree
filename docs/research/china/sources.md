### C01

**Jingjing, Xi'an stele (781), arrival narrative.** Edited English translation
in Charles F. Horne, ed., *The Sacred Books and Early Literature of the East*
(1917), XII, pp. 381-392, hosted by the
[Fordham Internet East Asian History Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/eastasia/781nestorian.asp).
Locator: main inscription, paragraph beginning "In the time of the accomplished
Emperor Tai-tsung," especially the sentence dating Olopun's arrival to 635.
Read this paragraph, the following edict, doctrinal paragraphs and colophon.
Supports a **781 report of a 635 arrival**, not an extant document written in 635.
Church patronage/eulogy perspective; old English renderings of names and
theological terms require care. Do not adopt Horne's introductory degeneration
theory or his disparaging evaluations as historical explanation.

### C02

Same source/edition as C01. Locator: main inscription, "In the seventh month of
the year A.D. 638," followed by the proclamation permitting dissemination and
ordering a church with twenty-one priests in the capital. The imperial
proclamation is **embedded in later testimony**. Supports the reported
authorization, not a measured empire-wide conversion or proof of a surviving
original 638 edict. C01/C02/C03 are one inscription, not three witnesses.

### C03

Same source/edition as C01. Locators: doctrinal opening; paragraph beginning
"Twenty-seven sacred books"; end of the ode and dated erection colophon
("2d year of Kien-chung"); following Syriac inscriptions.
Supports a 781 monument, Christian ritual/doctrinal self-description and
Chinese/Syriac presentation. Retain the **year**, not a newly converted modern
calendar day. "Triune" terminology in this old translation does not settle
Christological classification. No Christian population can be inferred from its
panegyric claims of geographic reach.

### C04

**National Museum of China, 大秦景教流行中国碑拓片**, undated collection description.
[Chinese museum record](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gjwxbt/201912/t20191211_179522.shtml).
Locator: explanatory paragraph beginning "大秦，即东罗马帝国", sentences dating
arrival to 贞观九年（635） and naming 景净 as author.
Read the Chinese descriptive paragraph; confirms the institutional catalog's
identification/name and interpretation of the monument. This is **not independent
contemporary corroboration** of the stele's arrival story. Do not repeat its
compressed account of a 428 separation as a sufficient history of the Church
of the East, or treat "Daqin" as proof of direct Byzantine jurisdiction.
The website's copyright notice remains in force; no rubbing is reproduced.

### C05

**John of Monte Corvino, report dated 1305-01-08.**
[Fordham edited letter](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/source/corvino1.asp).
Edition identified on page: Henry Yule, revised Henri Cordier, *Cathay and the
Way Thither*, 2nd ed. (1914), III, pp. 45-51, abridged via Barnard and Hodges
(1958), pp. 107-108. Locators: first paragraph (departure from Tauris in 1291);
"I have built a church"; "Also I have gradually bought"; "I have got a competent
knowledge"; final dating clause. Entire displayed letter read.
Supports Franciscan presence, building/translation claims and its testimony date.
The approximate baptism total is the author's estimate, not a census.
Do not sanitize the report of buying children or infer voluntary conversion
for every participant. The editorial preface's 1307 archbishop date is secondary,
not contained in the 1305 letter.

### C06

**Kangxi decree of 1692**, edited translation at

Locator: "From Decree of K'ang-hsi (1692)," from Stephen Neill,

Read all three displayed document extracts. This extract supports the decree's
permission/protection language, not uniform local enforcement.

### C07

Same page as C06: **Clement XI decree (1715)**, numbered provisions I-V and
the final qualification about permissible customs. Edition: Dan J. Li, trans.,
*China in Transition, 1517-1911* (1969), pp. 22-24.
Supports restrictions on Confucian/ancestral rites and divine terminology,
not a blanket ban on all Chinese customs. Distinguish decree from sourcebook
editorial commentary judging which side was right; that commentary is not
adopted. This is an edited translation, not a fresh inspection of the papal
original or its entire legal history.

### C08

Same page: **Kangxi decree (1721)**, opening "Reading this proclamation";
Li (1969), p. 22. Supports the emperor's stated rejection and prohibition on
preaching. Not sufficient by itself to date or explain Yongzheng's separate
1724 enforcement; see C24. Three documents share one modern editorial pipeline.

### C26

Same source as C25. Locators: opening paragraphs; "Challenges of measuring
Christianity in China"; "Recent change in Christian measures";
"Government estimates"; "Claims by advocacy groups"; "Are Catholics declining
as a share of China's population?"
Supports the comparison of 2018 CGSS ~2%/~20 million adult self-identifiers
with CFPS ~7%/~81 million believing in Jesus and/or Tianzhu; 2010/2018 CGSS
difference not statistically significant; official 2018 Protestant 38 million;
Asia Harvest 2020 all-age Protestant 109.7 million, as **reported and critiqued
by Pew**. We did not independently verify advocacy rosters or government totals.
Do not treat multiple series reproduced by Pew as independent new fieldwork.

Audit extension: also read "Geographic distribution of churches," the full
"Claims by advocacy groups" discussion of migration and CFPS reinterviews,
and the Catholic-decline section. The 2004 venue census is **registered sites,
not persons or a current map**. Among 2012 CFPS adult Christian identifiers,
about a quarter no longer identified in 2016; among 2016 identifiers, about a
third had not identified in 2012. These conditional denominators differ.
Pew also reports that 72% of 2018 CGSS Christian identifiers were women.
Its qualitative rural/urban discussion is secondary reporting, not a measured
national migration effect. Read with C27; do not turn responses into known
individual conversion dates, permanent retention rates or private rosters.
