# China: question-led research audit

## Independent baseline, before packet comparison

Researcher: Copilot writer, 2026-11-24. This baseline was written after external
passage reading and before opening the China research packet or public dataset.
It is a writer's audit, not independent scholarly approval. The questions below
were not derived from field counts or the existing roster. Source IDs and exact
repository mappings are supplied in the comparison below; this baseline retains
the initial answers rather than replacing them with what the repository contains.

Boundary: Christian histories in the changing polities occupying present mainland
China, with historically necessary Inner Asian, maritime, Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan
and diaspora connections, not an assertion of timeless borders or sovereignty.
Earliest surviving evidence through selected twentieth- and early twenty-first-
century evidence; retrieval 2026-11-24 is not coverage through 2026. Contemporary
personal rosters and meeting details are excluded. Catholic, Church of the East,
Eastern Orthodox, Protestant and indigenous/renewal histories are separate units.
No unattested Oriental Orthodox institutional tree is required.

| Question | Independently researched answer and inspected passage |
| --- | --- |
| Q01. What does "arrival" mean, and what did early Christians actually do? | The 781 stele retrospectively dates Alopen's arrival to 635 and an edict to 638. Its baptism, fasting, daily prayer and weekly worship account is Christian self-presentation, not an impartial census. Read the translated inscription from "Twenty-seven sacred books" through the arrival/edict and dated colophon in Fordham's *Nestorian Tablet* (Horne 1917, pp. 381-392). The editor's account of degeneration is a separate, prejudicial modern interpretation, not the inscription's evidence. |
| Q02. Was medieval Christianity only a foreign arrival followed by disappearance? | The Syriac *History of Rabban Sawma*, Budge translation, chapters I-III (Bedjan text pp. 3-13), describes Christian households, ecclesiastical learning, monastic choices and outward pilgrimage from Khanbaliq and Kawshang. This supplies agency and ordinary religious formation beyond European visitors. It does not prove a continuous local institution from Tang times. Kawshang's identification is uncertain even in the edition. |
| Q03. Did different medieval traditions coexist, and what explains interruption? | Marignolli's retrospective account, Yule edition pp. 210-216, describes Franciscan churches, an archbishop's residence and imperial support in Cambalec. Read against the distinct Syriac narrative, not as evidence all Christians belonged to the Latin mission. Neither passage establishes an exact extinction date or a single cause of disappearance. |
| Q04. How did women and village households sustain Catholic life? | Michel Chambon, *Religions* 10 (2019), 447, introduction and section 1.1, explains beatas living within families, teaching women, baptizing and caring for the sick; Petronilla Chen's 1643 vows are a particular example. His 2015-2017 ethnography and historical synthesis challenge treating European-style convents or clergy as the whole church. No sensitive contemporary case identities need be reproduced. |
| Q05. What changed mission methods, and whose interests did they serve? | BDCC Taylor, "The China Inland Mission is born," describes the 1865 nondenominational inland mission; "The Lammermuir party" names Wang Laijun's 1867 ordination. BDCC Richard, "Famine relief and afterwards" and "1891-1910," describes famine relief, educational reform and publishing, including disagreement over method and theology. The latter's header says 1915 but its narrative says death in 1919: do not copy the header. These evangelical biographies are not independent verdicts on imperialism or orthodoxy. |
| Q06. Were women only missionaries' assistants? | BDCC Moon, paragraphs beginning "In 1879," "Trying to arouse," and "Still struggling," describes rural Shandong itineration, women's hymn/doctrine classes and local invitations to teach men. BDCC Dora Yu, 1909 school and 1920 Fuzhou paragraphs, describes a Chinese woman training preachers and influencing Nee. Both accounts have devotional framing; "first woman" and heroic conversion totals require separate evidence. |
| Q07. What did local theology and worship produce beyond translated doctrine? | Guo Dengjie and Wang Lina, *Religions* 15 (2024), 168, sections 3.2.1, 4.2.1-4.2.2, examines Ningbo-language notation, six churches' 1936 *Hymns of Universal Praise*, and Chao Tzu-ch'en/Hu De'ai's rural devotional hymn. Cooperation on worship is not a denominational merger. The article's hymn counts are internally inconsistent (452 + 62 does not equal 512); do not admit those totals. |
| Q08. What is lost by treating "Chinese" as one language or ethnicity? | UBS China Partnership, "Miao Bible - 106 Years of Waiting" (2009), "The History" and "Support," reports Big Flowery Miao translation, 1917 NT, a 1990 local OT team and 2009 full Bible, using Chinese versions and original-language checking. Its missionary-only script-origin shorthand must be qualified: BDCC Pollard, early mass-movement and translation discussions, names Chinese coworker Stephen Lee and Miao evangelists/teachers. Neither supports equating this variety with every Miao/Hmong language. |
| Q09. What made independent/renewal communities distinct? | Chinese-language BDCC Jing Dianying, paragraphs on 1921 cooperative, 1927 naming, collective work/prayer and 1952 suppression, describes Pentecostal communal life, not simply another denomination. It also mentions accusations of authoritarian leadership without adjudicating them. BDCC Sung, ministry periods 1-4 and "China's John the Baptist," describes repentance, lay training, music and transborder itineration rather than a new corporate church. |
| Q10. How did Christians negotiate state power and dissent among themselves? | BDCC Wang, "After 1949" and "Long imprisonment," attributes his rejection of TSPM to doctrine and ecclesial independence; his own self-support did not equal TSPM membership. Ding's October 1980 opening address (reprinted in *Tian Feng* 1981/1; Chinese CCC/TSPM republication 2020) defends patriotism and self-government as theological necessities. Preserve both voices; neither proves all nonparticipants unpatriotic or all participants insincere. |
| Q11. Did post-Mao rebuilding include substantive theology and training? | WCC's 2012 Ting obituary identifies seminary, CCC and TSPM leadership and ecumenical re-entry. Chen Yilu's Chinese-language 2019 CCC/TSPM address locates the November 1998 theological reconstruction decision and presents cosmic Christ, divine love and reconciliation. These are institutional advocacy and commemoration; disagreement about coerced accommodation remains open. |
| Q12. What did war do to institutions and women beyond martyr biographies? | Yale-hosted Smith archive historical overview, "The History of Ginling College," identifies five sponsoring mission boards, Wu Yi-fang's 1928 presidency, 1938 exile to Chengdu and 1951 combination with University of Nanking. This is inspected archivists' narrative, not inspection of the underlying letters. Financial dependence, Chinese governance and wartime displacement are different relations from descent. |
| Q13. Was Orthodoxy a single unchanged Russian jurisdiction? | Dionisy Pozdnyaev, *Orthodoxy in China* (1998), chapter V, paragraphs on November 23, 1956, May 30, 1957 and the unheld council, separates Moscow's autonomy decision, Vasily's consecration and contested leadership with Simeon. Emigration, property transfers and suppression also shaped decline. This Moscow-associated account's polemical character needs an opposing jurisdictional check. |
| Q14. Do totals establish conversion, growth or retention? | Pew, *Measuring Religion in China* (2023), chapter 4 opening, "Recent change" and "Traits," distinguishes 2018 CGSS adult identification (~2%, ~20m) from CFPS belief and practice; the 2010/2018 difference is not statistically significant. Methods, "CGSS" and "CFPS," identify sampling exclusions, reweighting, changing questions and missing religion variables. A non-significant difference is neither proof of no change nor permission for an exponential-growth narrative. |
| Q15. Which experiences and explanations remain difficult to establish? | Chambon introduction contrasts local appropriation models; Guo/Wang frames hymnody as glocalization; BDCC biographies often judge success by conversion and doctrinal fidelity; CCC/TSPM sources emphasize patriotic reconstruction. These are different perspectives, not mutually independent proofs of the same national narrative. County-level lay life, Catholic dissent, coercion within churches, minority languages beyond one example, and reliable retention series need specifically targeted follow-up. |

### Post-comparison research and access decisions

All work below was done on 2026-11-24, after the retained baseline; it supplements,
not replaces, those initial answers.

- Read Covell's full Taylor entry (C45) and Stanley's full Richard entry (C48),
  tracing their printed dictionary metadata. These improve specialist context
  but share mission historiography with BDCC; two websites are not necessarily
  two independent evidentiary chains.
- Read Richard's own 1916 dedication/introduction (C59, pp. 7-8). It confirms
  his explicit civilizational framing, not the objective necessity or success
  of that program. Did not pretend to read the rest of the memoir.
- Read Pew's actual CFPS reinterview and geographic-distribution passages
  (C26), not just search results; retained D07-D09 with the relevant universes,
  dates and limitations. No underlying respondent records accessed.
- Reopened Sawma's household/formation passage, Sung's ministry sections,
  Moon's biography, the WCC obituary, UBS Miao article, Chambon's historical
  section, Pozdnyaev's chapter and the Ginling overview to check the narrower
  claims. Source access failures on later CCC/TSPM retries do not erase the
  earlier successful Chinese passage reading; C53/C54 record that distinction.
- Tried Franzmann's publisher HTML/XML/PDF, an author-associated academic-host
  page and a discovered full-text repository link; none yielded inspectable
  full text. Quanzhou's bilingual epitaph interpretation is **not admitted**.
  Sawma repairs a different substantive Yuan gap; it does not resolve epigraphy.
- Tried King's Candida Xu PDF; it returned a failed site shell. Harrison's
  village book remains abstract-only. Chambon's read historical synthesis is
  useful, but its references are not a claim that King, Harrison or their
  underlying sources were directly inspected.
- Sought opposing Orthodox evidence: ROCOR Studies, "St. John of Shanghai and
  Commemoration of the Patriarch of Moscow" (2024-07-31) and "The ROCOR and the
  Axis Powers during World War II Part 2. Metropolitanate District in China"
  (2021-05-11). Discovered references to 1945 jurisdiction correspondence,
  but repeated actual retrieval failed DNS/transport. Search excerpts are
  **leads only**; specific next task is to inspect the letters/editions and
  compare commemoration, jurisdiction and later departure, not accept one
  retrospective assignment of blame.
- Searched the 1939 rites instruction/1946 hierarchy. An accessible
  [Hong Kong study PDF](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/T155_11.pdf)
  downloaded but yielded no readable extracted text; no page was visually
  inspected. Unseen PDF/search statements are not proof. U17 still requests
  the official acts and Chinese reception rather than invented dates/consensus.

## Re-audit after remediation

**Result:** the six starting leads now have supported, historically differentiated
research: Taylor E49/H59-H60/C44-C45; Moon E52/H62/C46;
Richard E53/H61/H63/C47-C48/C59; Wang E57/H72/H75-H77/C49;
Sung E55/H69-H70/C50; Ding E59/H78-H79/C52-C54. Their inclusion is not the
completion metric: Yuan household formation, beatas, local hymnody, Miao
translators, Wu/Ginling, internal power and retention were independently
identified and repaired as well.

The register now has H01-H79, D01-D09, E01-E72, R01-R42, C01-C59 and S01-S55;
original IDs are stable. Dates remain vow/publication/appointment/building/
arrest/speech/decision or retrospective period dates, not fabricated institutional
births. Every newly admitted historical episode is present in chronology,
entities, citations and synthesis; the audit does not stand in for those records.
Every relationship is descriptive/qualified, not an inferred corporate genealogy.
