# Christianity in China: research foundation

**Collected/as of 2026-11-24. Research only; not a published dataset.**
The evidence below supports an introductory, multi-tradition foundation, not a
complete history or an unbroken Chinese church genealogy. The last dated
institutional event verified here is the agreement renewal of 2024-10-22;
this is not a claim that nothing changed in 2025-2026.

## Scope and geography

Begin with the earliest securely documented Christian presence in the Chinese
historical record: the Tang Church of the East, reported arriving in 635 by a
monument erected in 781. The two dates are not contemporary corroboration.
Earlier apostolic-arrival traditions are not admitted as established events.
Follow major encounters, ruptures, indigenous developments and institutional
reorganizations through the PRC and modern period. Do not begin Christianity
with either Matteo Ricci or Robert Morrison.

The principal geography is the territory of today's mainland China, interpreted
through contemporary Tang, Yuan, Ming, Qing, Republican and PRC contexts, not
as a state with invariant borders. Chang'an/Xi'an, Cambalec/Beijing,
Canton/Guangzhou, Shanghai, Shandong and the northeast occur as different
historical settings. Yuan imperial networks extended beyond present borders;
not every Mongol Christian was Chinese, and not every Mongol-ruled place
belongs in this country packet. Qing-Russian border contacts and Albazin are
cross-border context, not a retrospective territorial claim.

Macao/Macau is included as a Catholic entry and jurisdictional hub, and
Hong Kong as a publishing, archival and connective context. Their colonial and
later distinct administrative settings must not be collapsed into mainland
policy. Malacca is an external mission/printing node needed to explain Liang
Fa, not a location inside China. Taiwan's separate church histories, Hong
Kong's complete denominational history, overseas Chinese churches, Korea,
Japan, Mongolia outside the present mainland, and Central Asian churches are
out of scope except for specifically evidenced contacts. This is an analytical
boundary, not a position on sovereignty. Tibet/Xizang, Xinjiang and ethnic
minority Christian histories are geographically relevant but inadequately
researched here; they are gaps, not evidence of absence.

Church of the East, Catholic, Protestant (mission, ecumenical, revivalist and
independent), and Eastern Orthodox histories are covered at differing depths.
"Christianity" is not limited to PRC administrative categories: Catholicism
and Protestantism are separately recognized there. "Jidujiao" can designate
Christianity broadly or Protestantism in administrative usage; translate by
context. No detailed census of Oriental Orthodox, Armenian, or other smaller
historical presences has been completed. Do not infer their nonexistence.

## How to read evidence status

Chronology status **P** means the writer read the identified primary/edited
primary passage; **S** means a secondary passage was read; **Q** means the
record remains explicitly qualified/conflicted. These describe source access,
not independent approval or a numerical probability. A P record can contain
retrospective, interested or translated testimony. Claims about what a document
says are different from claims that everything it says happened.

Citation IDs C01-C39 resolve to exact locators in the source index. Repeated
citations within one index entry do not create independent witnesses.
Dates are Common Era, retaining source-supported precision. Uncertain ranges
are not continuous institutional lifespans. Chinese characters are included
where established by inspected Chinese material or scholarship; aliases
without a cited Chinese form remain in their attested Latin spelling.

No scans, photographs, book pages, bulk translations, contemporary community
rosters or identifying meeting-location data are part of this deliverable.
