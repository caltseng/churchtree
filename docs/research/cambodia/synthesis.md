# Christianity in Cambodia: an introductory synthesis

Read [scope](README.md), [passage index](sources.md) and [coverage](coverage.md)
with this account. Citation IDs identify exact inspected passages, not a claim
that every cited author is neutral or that their underlying archives were read.

## Encounters before a national church, 1555-1850

The opening evidence here is a Portuguese Dominican mission, not an indigenous
Christian nation's foundation. Mam's open chapter summary dates Gaspar da Cruz's
visit to 1555-1556 and presents his own pessimistic account of conversion under
royal and religious authority. His description of Cambodian religion is a
missionary interpretation, not the packet's neutral classification. The full
travel narrative and original terminology remain to be checked.
[C01, Mam summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/christianity-in-east-and-southeast-asia/cambodia/54DB743DF8AF47C359C1EF1DBB943FAE).

By Chevreul's arrival in 1665 the setting already included Portuguese and
Vietnamese refugees and other clergy. The Catholic encyclopedia locates
communities around Colompé/Phnom Penh and Udong, including people displaced from
Makassar and Cochinchina. Lay migration is therefore not a footnote to a single
European missionary's "arrival." Its account of Chevreul's 1670 detention shows
competing Portuguese patronage and MEP authority; it does not establish a merger
or an unchanged national Catholic corporation. [C02, Early History](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/cambodia-catholic-church).

Nicolas/Nicholas Levasseur's Khmer catechetical translations in 1768-1777
indicate an effort beyond Portuguese/Vietnamese pastoral networks. The evidence
does not identify every collaborator, reader, print run or surviving manuscript,
and does not demonstrate uninterrupted expansion. Catholic sacramental,
catechetical and episcopal institutions should not be mistaken for a universal
Christian confession shared by all later traditions.
[C03, Early History](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/cambodia-catholic-church).

## Colonial borders, migration and language, 1850-1965

The 1850 vicariate included part of Laos. The French protectorate began in
1863, and eight Vietnamese provinces entered its ecclesiastical territory in
1865. Catholic history predates colonial protection, but later migration and
pastoral priorities were entangled with the protectorate's economic and political
order. In 1955 the Vietnamese territory was separated as Cantho jurisdiction.
Counts spanning these changes cannot be graphed as one constant-territory national
series. Independence in 1953 was a political transition, not automatic autonomy
for every church. [C03-C04, Early History](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/cambodia-catholic-church).

Alliance institutional history dates the arrival of the Hammond and Ellison
families to 1923; Wong restores the names Esther Hammond and Muriel Ellison and
the role of Cambodian Buddhist and Christian language consultants. Phnom Penh
translation and Battambang training were different kinds of work. The
Battambang Bible Institute's first term is dated September 1, 1925 by the
centennial account. It distinguishes translation completion in 1952 from
distribution of the complete Hammond Bible in 1954. OMF's 2021 history
(updated January 2022) instead calls 1953 the whole-Bible publication year.
Those might describe different acts, but original title pages/imprints have
not been inspected: this packet does not declare them reconciled or replace
distribution with an unverified first-printing date (C43/KH55).
This is a Protestant
milestone, not Christianity's first Cambodian presence.
[C05, Alliance](https://awf.world/awf-news/a-century-of-gospel-proclamation-in-cambodia/);
[C06, Wong p.28](https://doi.org/10.46567/ijt.v9i1.170).

The national Alliance organization became independent of the mission in 1954
and adopted Khmer Evangelical Church in 1957, according to its own account.
Ros Hom and other Cambodian evangelists were actors rather than passive
recipients of foreign work; a former soldier's cross-border experience did not
make the Thai army a church ancestor. The institutional constitutions,
exact transfer of authority and broader denominational competition need more
primary research. [C07 and S03, local evangelists paragraph](https://awf.world/awf-news/a-century-of-gospel-proclamation-in-cambodia/).

Women's contributions were not restricted to foreign missionaries' marriages.
Wong's archival analysis identifies a 1960 women's group in Kompong Thom
supporting Mrs. Nai, and Nai's subsequent ministry with Muriel Ellison. The
long-term Bible school opened to women in 1961, after short-term training had
already become coeducational. These are institutional differences, not a story
of universal female exclusion followed by one national emancipation date.
[C08, Wong pp.30-31](https://doi.org/10.46567/ijt.v9i1.170).

## War, indigenous persistence and destruction, 1965-1990

Removal of American missionaries in 1965 did not end Cambodian evangelical
life. Wong and Alliance history describe local persistence through restrictions
and renewed activity after 1970. Keam Ny/Sumido's Khmer broadcasts from Manila
in 1974-1975 and women-led correspondence instruction documented in a June 1975
report show women teaching across boundaries despite restrictions on formal
office. A report's date is not the department's founding date.
[C09, Alliance](https://awf.world/awf-news/a-century-of-gospel-proclamation-in-cambodia/);
[C06/C12-C13, Wong pp.28, 32-34](https://doi.org/10.46567/ijt.v9i1.170).

The 1968 Catholic prefectures and Tep Im Sotha's role in Battambang mark
indigenous institutional leadership. Anti-Vietnamese violence and deportation
in 1970 severely reduced Catholic communities. That is an ethnic and political
rupture, not a doctrinal church split. The encyclopedia's roughly 61,000 Catholic
total and ethnic subdivisions are approximate mission-era counts (the listed
subtotals do not exactly sum), not a denominationally neutral census.
[C04/C10, Catholic encyclopedia](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/cambodia-catholic-church).

Scheer's abstract places some Bunong recruits' early-1970s conversions in army
displacement to Phnom Penh and encounters with Khmer preachers. This complicates
the idea that every highland conversion was a rejection of majority identity or
a uniform "modernization" strategy. Only the abstract was accessible; no detailed
life histories or universal claim about Bunong religion is imported.
[C11, Scheer abstract](https://hal.science/hal-04042034v1).

Salas's episcopal consecration preceded the Khmer Rouge capture of Phnom Penh.
The encyclopedia gives April 15, 1975, while the local Catholic community
history gives April 14 (C39). The public record now retains a two-day uncertain
window, not a two-day ceremony or a silently selected day. His death under the
regime is not in question here, but
the inspected sources give 1976 and 1977; the timeline does not silently select
one. Christians suffered destruction of schools and churches, forced labor,
hunger and killing during 1975-1979. These events belong within broader
violence against Buddhists, Muslims, ethnic minorities and the population at
large, not a story that makes Christians the sole victims or assigns everyone
the same legal category of genocide.
[C14, encyclopedia](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/cambodia-catholic-church);
[C19, Vatican News opening](https://www.vaticannews.va/it/chiesa/news/2025-07/cambogia-fede-chiesa-cattolici-giovani.html);
[C15, Wong pp.28-29](https://doi.org/10.46567/ijt.v9i1.170);
[C16, Yale context](https://macmillan.yale.edu/gsp/research-collection/cambodian-genocide-program).

Christians survived within Cambodia and in Thai-border camps; later returnees
and diaspora links were consequential. Wong describes these diverse routes
without reducing belief to material inducement or one psychological response.
The Vietnamese overthrow of the Khmer Rouge in 1979 did not immediately create
full Christian freedom. The blanket NCE phrase that the subsequent regime
suppressed "all forms" of religion is not adopted as an account of all Buddhism
or the entire decade; the narrower supported claim is continuing Christian
restrictions and actual worship until public reopening around 1990.
[C15, Wong pp.28-29](https://doi.org/10.46567/ijt.v9i1.170);
[C21, Catholic reopening](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/cambodia-catholic-church);
[C25, Alliance persistence](https://awf.world/awf-news/a-century-of-gospel-proclamation-in-cambodia/).

The Alliance account specifically describes a surviving pastor, rendered
Yourng South and Yourng Soth on the same page, visiting scattered believers.
That account cannot be combined uncritically with OMF's assertion that all
leaders died or fled. OMF's "killed or fled" estimate and the Alliance's killed
estimate also measure different outcomes, not competing samples to average.
Both are mission narratives, not verified mortality surveys (C43-C44).

## Reconstruction, service, texts and plural institutions

The interconfessional translation team had lost Khmer colleagues under the
Khmer Rouge. UBS testimony describes renewed work in France in 1985, involving
Ponchaud and Arun Sok Nhep, followed by return and publication of the Modern
Khmer Bible in 1997. This is collaboration across confessional boundaries, not
a Catholic-Protestant merger. Translation choices and reception need fuller Khmer
textual study; publication does not prove that the Bible alone caused numerical
growth. [C17-C18, UBS mirror](https://www.biblesociety.sg/modern-khmer-bible-at-the-heart-of-the-revival-of-the-cambodian-church/).
Wong's pp.25-26 add reception rather than another founding date: some
evangelical interlocutors remained attached to KHOV, and a Khmer-song
repertoire using indigenous tunes is associated with Barnabas Mam and Sarin
Sam. This is not a universal worship inventory or a checked publication year
for their collection. Footnote 9 reverses the adjacent version wording, so
no conclusion about which version readers condemned is taken from it (C46).
The eBible KSV title/rights page adds Central Khmer and 1997/2005 copyright
metadata, but is not an inspected printing colophon or a textual comparison
(C45/KH56). Neither edition labels nor translation advocacy establish uptake.

Catholic women's reconstruction also occurred through work, friendship,
teaching and vocation, not only male clerical replacement. De Poli's 2023
account follows Bun Nath's marketplace conversations and Ang Songvat/Marie
and Bun Tharin/Teresa's catechesis, sewing/literacy teaching with Thai sisters,
family expectations about marriage, 2002 novitiate and 2004 first profession.
The local church history also dates two Cambodian Lovers of the Cross first
vows to 2004. These accounts support a bounded formation history, not De
Poli's sweeping assertion of the first Christian in a supposedly empty
postwar church. Older survivors are already attested. Current residences and
group membership are not republished.
[C39-C40](sources.md#claim-specific-passage-index)

Christian reconstruction was also educational, medical and social, not just
clerical replacement. Salesian accounts distinguish Thai-camp vocational work
in 1989, approval in May 1991, technical instruction in September and Sisters'
training for women in 1992. Cambodian instructors and young workers belong in
that story. JRS's 1990 agreement involved women religious and a lay volunteer
alongside Jesuits; later service included disability support and landmine
advocacy. Its institutional history identifies Denise Coghlan and Cambodian
disabled advocate Tun Channareth in the campaign awarded the 1997 Nobel Peace
Prize. Neither organizational success claims nor beneficiary counts are
independently evaluated here.
[C20/C23, Don Bosco](https://donboscocambodia.org/history/);
[C22/C36, Jesuit history](https://oldsite.jcapsj.org/about-us/about-the-jesuit-conference/our-provinces-regions-and-missions/jesuit-mission-in-cambodia/).

Ponchaud's January 2015 interview supplies a different kind of evidence:
he recalls villagers first refusing canal reconstruction because it evoked
Khmer Rouge labor, negotiating road work, and later undertaking irrigation
work in May 2000. Local refusal, memory, knowledge and bargaining complicate
a donor-to-passive-beneficiary success story. This remains his retrospective
account, not independently verified consent, labor safety, hydrological
benefits or a template for development practice. His numerical impact and
broad cultural/religious judgments are not adopted.
[C41, development questions](https://www.nonfiction.fr/article-7498-cambodge-entretien-avec-franois-ponchaud.htm)

Postwar Methodist beginnings include diaspora initiative from France and
Switzerland in 1992, followed by cooperation among five agencies: UMC Global
Ministries, Connexio, Korean Methodist Church, Methodist Missions Society of
Singapore, and World Federation of Chinese Methodist Churches. Their 2001
Khmer hymnal and January 2003 joint mission precede the 2018 denomination/
autonomy stage. These five world bodies did not cease to exist in one Cambodian
merger. Global Ministries describes health, agriculture, education, women's
economic empowerment and pandemic relief; these are attributed program accounts,
not independent impact evaluations. [C24/C29/C35, mission history](https://umccambodia.org/history/);
[C38/S17, denomination and community programs](https://umcmission.org/story/come-visit-the-young-vibrant-methodist-community-in-cambodia/).

LDS official history dates recognition to February 26, 1994, including a Khmer
edition of that account. The tradition's expatriate, refugee/diaspora,
Vietnamese-speaking and Cambodian connections cannot be mapped as an Alliance
split. Modern Eastern Orthodox history is likewise a distinct immigrant and
cross-border story: its institutional page gives pastoral oversight from
Thailand in 2001 and registration in 2013, not descent from Catholic or Tang
Church of the East missions. [C27, English/Khmer chronology](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/history/global-histories/cambodia/kh-chronology?lang=khm);
[C28, Orthodox history](https://orthodoxchurchcambodia.org/orthodoxy-in-cambodia/).
The regional Moscow history further distinguishes the October 21, 2016
reassignment of Cambodia/Laos parishes from the Thailand deanery and the
February 26, 2019 establishment of a diocese across four countries. These are
ecclesiastical reorganizations, not new Cambodian state registrations or
proof that every Orthodox community shares this jurisdiction. Original
synodal acts and non-Moscow/local reception remain uninspected (C42).

Wong's 2019 fieldwork makes independent/charismatic congregations and women
leaders visible while documenting different rules for ordination. Her
pseudonymized interlocutors are not converted into public people/church nodes.
Foursquare and Pentecostal growth claims need denominational and independent
verification; a claim of nearly a million members cited critically by Wong
must not be treated as a measured national fact.
[C30, Wong pp.29-30, 34](https://doi.org/10.46567/ijt.v9i1.170).

## Freedom, numbers and the observation horizon

Article 43 of the historical 1993 constitutional text combines conditional
religious freedom with Buddhism as state religion. Recognition and toleration
are not identical to unrestricted public proselytism. USDOS's 2023 reporting
describes registration and restrictions on public proselytism and material
inducements. Humanitarian action must not be narrated as proof that recipients
converted voluntarily, or that conversion was always bought.
[C26, constitutional text](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/icl/cb00000_.html);
[C33, report sections I-II](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2111837.html).

No comparable demographic growth series has been established. USDOS juxtaposes
a 2020 World Religion Database Christian estimate (2.8% of population), a Catholic
estimate (0.4%) and NGO Protestant estimates (under 2%), with different definitions
and reference bases. The NIS 2019 variable page gives 0.3% among cases in its file
but explicitly warns these are not population summary statistics. Alliance
membership including children, Methodist full membership and average attendance
are different denominators. Do not average them, add them across time, infer a
2019 collapse, extrapolate annual growth or display an unqualified national
Christian count. Obtain the census questionnaire, full table and demographic
methods before quantifying change.
[C31, NIS warning](https://nada.nis.gov.kh/index.php/catalog/39/variable/V150);
[C33, demography](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2111837.html);
[S17, distinct membership/attendance](https://umcmission.org/story/come-visit-the-young-vibrant-methodist-community-in-cambodia/).

The 2023 evangelical centennial commemorated the 1923 Protestant mission; it
was not Christianity's hundredth anniversary in Cambodia. The latest selected
institutional event is the Holy See's June 28, 2025 coadjutor appointment of
Pierre Suon Hangly. Appointment does not establish an episcopal ordination day
or completed succession, and a 2025 endpoint does not imply nothing happened
afterward. The separate coverage register remains necessary.
[C32, centennial](https://awf.world/awf-news/a-century-of-gospel-proclamation-in-cambodia/);
[C34, primary appointment](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/bollettino/pubblico/2025/06/28/250628c.html).
