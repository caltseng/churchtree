## Boundary and historical units

The principal boundary is present-day Cambodia, not the entire former Khmer
Empire and not a timeless ethnic nation. The earliest mission admitted here is
Gaspar da Cruz's reported 1555-1556 visit, not a claim that no earlier Christian
ever entered the region. Portuguese maritime networks, Makassar, Siam/Thailand,
Cochinchina/southern Vietnam, France and refugee destinations are included only
where necessary to explain evidenced Cambodian connections.

Colonial ecclesiastical jurisdiction crossed today's border: the Cambodia
vicariate incorporated eight Vietnamese provinces in 1865, separated in 1955.
Its membership totals therefore cannot automatically be treated as counts
inside present Cambodia. Kampuchea/Cambodia are contextual state names;
Democratic Kampuchea (1975-1979), the subsequent Vietnamese-backed government,
and the restored kingdom are distinct political settings. Phnom Penh also
appears as Colompé in an early account; Udong/Oudong, Kompong/Kampong,
Battam-bang/Battambang, Ta Khmau/Takhmao and Cochin China/Cochinchina are
retained as source variants, not different institutions. Kampuchea Krom denotes
an external historical/cultural region in southern Vietnam, not territory
silently assigned to today's Cambodia.

Covered traditions include Catholic, evangelical/Alliance, Methodist,
Pentecostal/charismatic and independent Protestant contexts, Eastern Orthodox
and Latter-day Saint self-identified Christian history. They are not one lineage.
The Church of the East and Oriental Orthodox traditions were searched for but
not established by the admitted evidence; this is **missing research**, not proof
of absence. Full Thailand/Vietnam church histories, every mission/congregation,
and an exhaustive overseas Cambodian history are intentionally out of scope.
