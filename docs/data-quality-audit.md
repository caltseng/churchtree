# ChurchTree data-quality audit

Audit date: 2026-11-13 (America/Los_Angeles). Scope: the working-tree JSON in all 13 `data/sets/*` directories and `data/common`, with supporting architecture, collection, audit, and notes documentation. This is an audit of an actively changing working tree, not a committed release. The initial audit was read-only. The user subsequently authorized dataset-wide enrichment and verified historical corrections; the follow-up section below records those changes and supersedes the baseline counts and unresolved-reference findings. Unrelated existing modifications were preserved.

## Assessment

The graph is structurally usable but is not yet a consistently sourced historical reference. Its most serious risks are misleading institutional lineage, dates that combine different kinds of milestones, and apparent citation coverage that does not establish support for individual claims. Repairing source bundling is necessary, but will not resolve these content issues.

Priority meanings: **P1** affects historical interpretation or traceability and should precede publication as a reliable reference; **P2** affects completeness, comparability, or maintainability. “Confirmed” below means a reproducible local defect or a specific discrepancy supported by the linked external evidence. “Review” means the representation needs investigation or an explicit modeling decision; it is not permission to replace a historical claim automatically.

## Method and limits

All available JSON arrays were parsed and combined in memory, including shared and set-local citations/sources. Checks covered required keys, integer years, duplicate IDs within entity collections, edge endpoints, event links, citation references including nested key dates, citation-to-source references, directed cycles, isolated nodes, basic temporal consistency, source composition, and direct citation attachment. The audit also read the complete edge descriptions and node dates/statuses and sampled explanatory notes and detailed Presbyterian records.

The snapshot contained 63 nodes, 70 edges, 37 events, 71 sources, 180 citations, and an empty tag array. These counts describe raw source JSON, not `data/data.js`. A temporary inventory used for the checks is under `.tmp/data-audit/`; it is diagnostic material, not a canonical dataset or required deliverable.

External verification was a purposive sample of high-impact dates, parentage, naming, demographic scope, and disputed causation. It was not a random sample, a full fact-check of 170 graph records, or a complete HTTP/link audit of 71 source URLs. Official histories provide strong evidence of institutional dates and self-description, but their theological or causal assessments require attribution and corroboration. A successful page fetch does not establish that every claim attached to it is true. Failed retrieval by the research tool does not establish a dead URL.

The parent chat is fixing the previously identified omission of `data/common` sources and citations from the generated bundle. This report does not count that known implementation problem as a new historical-data defect, and does not assess the final explorer or validator being developed concurrently. Re-run counts and resolution checks after those changes settle.

## P1 — Reproducible integrity and provenance defects

### 1. Six citation references do not resolve — confirmed

None of the following IDs exists in the combined common and set-local citation arrays. Restoring common-source bundling alone cannot fix them.

| File | Record ID | Missing citation ID |
|---|---|---|
| `data/sets/adventist-us/edges.json` | `second-great-awakening-to-millerism-1831` | `cite-millerism-second-great-awakening` |
| `data/sets/baptist-europe/edges.json` | `church-of-england-to-english-separatists-1534` | `cite-church-of-england-separatists` |
| `data/sets/holiness-restorationist-us/edges.json` | `methodist-movement-to-holiness-movement-1850` | `cite-holiness-movement-methodism` |
| same | `holiness-movement-to-nazarene-1908` | `cite-nazarene-holiness-origins` |
| `data/sets/pentecostal-us/edges.json` | `holiness-movement-to-pentecostalism-1900` | `cite-pentecostalism-holiness-roots` |
| `data/sets/presbyterian-europe/edges.json` | `english-reformation-to-english-congregation-geneva-1555` | `cite-english-congregation-geneva-exiles` |

Remedy: research each actual relationship and add a precise source/citation, or remove the unsupported reference and mark the claim unresolved for review. Do not create placeholder citations that merely make a validator green.

### 2. Citation quantity substantially overstates support — confirmed

`data/common/sources.json` contains 65 Wikipedia sources; the six additional sources in `data/sets/presbyterian-us/sources.json` are denominational/history sites. Thus 65/71 sources (91.5%) and 162/180 citation records (90%) point to Wikipedia. Wikipedia can locate research leads, but these records are not independent corroboration of one another.

Eighty-six citation records are not referenced by nodes, edges, events, or their key dates in the JSON snapshot. Some may still serve Markdown notes; they should not be deleted without checking those consumers. Examples include `cite-sda-members`, `cite-elca-members`, `cite-catholic-church-members-2025`, and `cite-church-of-england-members-2024`: their existence in the registry does not attach evidence to the displayed size estimates.

Even the seven directly cited Presbyterian nodes do not map each distinctive to evidence. `pcusa.distinctives` includes `Barthianist`, while its attached citations describe formation, reunion, and moderators. A reunion source does not by itself establish a denomination-wide theological classification. Likewise, the PCA edge has citations for founding and participants, but its reasons include claims about Scripture and Christology that need separate support and attribution.

Remedy: build a claim inventory for dates, parentage, causes, distinctives, figures, and statistics. Attach claim-specific evidence or introduce field/claim references; retain explicit unsupported status until reviewed. Use official statistical reports and archival records for factual claims, and independent scholarship for contested causal interpretations.

### 3. Locator strings are not consistently reproducible — confirmed limitation

All 180 citations contain a locator and access date; all 180 resolve to a source. However, `data/sets/presbyterian-us/citations.json` uses locators such as `L502`, `L509`, and `L460-L462`. These are extraction-line references, not stable sections or anchors on the source pages. On this audit's fetch, the PCUSA historical page has only 86 extracted lines, and its first-General-Assembly passage appears at line 50. No archived extraction is recorded in the citation. Common citations such as `Infobox: members` are understandable but vulnerable to page edits and do not preserve the cited revision.

Remedy: record section titles/anchors, publication metadata, a short identifying passage or claim paraphrase, and stable revision/archive links when possible. Preserve access dates separately from observation years. Two existing source URLs (PCA December 4 history and PCUSA Heritage Sunday) could not be retrieved with the research tool; mark them for a later browser/link check rather than declaring them broken.

## P1 — Historical accuracy and relationship semantics

### 4. ELCA formation is recorded as 1987 without the effective 1988 distinction — verified discrepancy

In `data/sets/lutheran-us/nodes.json`, ELCA starts in 1987 and ALC/LCA/AELC end in 1987. The three `*-to-elca-1987` merger edges and the corresponding event in `events.json` describe merger/formation in 1987. The [ELCA's official history](https://www.elca.org/about-the-elca/who-we-are/history) says the church formed in 1988 and distinguishes the April 1987 constituting convention. Its [1987 transition record](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/CWA1987.pdf) schedules startup for January 1, 1988.

Proposed review: retain a 1987 constituting-convention milestone and use 1988 for effective merger/startup. Review predecessor end-year conventions together: an end year of 1987 can mean the final active calendar year, while an effective merger date is 1988. Do not mechanically replace every 1987 occurrence. Also review `scandinavian-churches-to-elca-1987` in `lutheran-europe/edges.json`, which describes heritage rather than a direct organizational origin.

### 5. Triennial-to-ABCUSA continuation collapses separate organizations and later names — verified discrepancy

`data/sets/baptist-us/edges.json`, record `triennial-to-abcusa-1907`, and `events.json`, record `event-abcusa-1907`, say the Triennial Convention reorganized as American Baptist Churches USA in 1907. `nodes.json` ends the Triennial body in 1907 with status `merged`. The [ABCUSA official history](https://www.abc-usa.org/our-history) instead describes the 1814 convention as a predecessor of the foreign-missions board, northern societies coordinating through the Northern Baptist Convention in 1907, and the ABCUSA name being adopted in 1972 (after the 1950 American Baptist Convention name).

Proposed review: separate the missionary-society lineage from the 1907 umbrella convention and add the 1950/1972 naming milestones. A modern label for a continuous body can be acceptable, but the current event and edge assert a specific reorganization directly into that label. Revise the Triennial end date/status only after archival confirmation.

### 6. Pentecostal origins precede their modeled parent — confirmed internal contradictions; institutional history verified

In `data/sets/pentecostal-us/edges.json`, `pentecostalism-to-cog-cleveland-1886` and `pentecostalism-to-cogic-1897` are `origin` edges from a movement whose node starts in 1906. Their prose now acknowledges earlier Holiness roots, but the typed graph still depicts formation from a future parent. `holiness-movement-to-pentecostalism-1900` also predates its target's 1906 start. `nbcusa-to-cogic-1896` predates its target's 1897 start.

The [Church of God's official history](https://churchofgod.org/about/a-brief-history-of-the-church-of-god/) documents an 1886 Christian Union beginning and later developments. [COGIC's official history](https://www.cogic.org/about-us/our-history/) distinguishes its 1897 name from Mason's 1907 Azusa experience; its [founder account](https://www.cogic.org/about-us/our-founder/) also describes the 1907 division over Pentecostal teaching. These sources support distinguishing organizational foundation, Holiness ancestry, and later Pentecostal identity. They do not, by themselves, establish the exact national-convention parent/date asserted by the 1896 edge.

Proposed review: model pre-Pentecostal foundations separately from adoption/influence milestones. Keep the 1896 national-convention split claim pending stronger evidence. Do not simply move well-supported 1886/1897 founding dates to 1906.

### 7. Other temporal warnings need relationship-specific rules — confirmed local warnings

| File | Edge ID | Warning |
|---|---|---|
| `data/sets/baptist-europe/edges.json` | `church-of-england-to-english-separatists-1534` | Child begins 1606; edge claims a split in 1534 |
| `data/sets/presbyterian-europe/edges.json` | `genevan-reformed-to-church-of-scotland-1559` | Influence precedes target's 1560 start |
| same | `english-congregation-geneva-to-presbyterian-polity-scotland-1555` | Influence precedes target's 1581 start |
| same | `genevan-reformed-to-presbyterian-polity-scotland-1541` | Influence precedes target's 1581 start |

Together with finding 6, these are eight temporal warnings. A formative influence can legitimately predate a target's constitution, so the three Presbyterian influence edges are not proven historical errors. The Separatist split is a stronger date/identity inconsistency: determine whether the node means the broad Separatist movement or a particular 1606 congregation. The node's generic “Believer's baptism” distinctive also risks projecting later Baptist identity onto Separatists generally; this remains a research question.

The model in `docs/architecture/data-model.md` lists only split/merge/origin/continuation, while three records use `influence`. Approve and document that type and its chronology explicitly. Add event-kind/date-precision conventions instead of making one integer represent founding, legal incorporation, census recognition, and cultural influence interchangeably.

### 8. Restoration splits are too instantaneous — verified qualification

`data/sets/holiness-restorationist-us/edges.json`, `disciples-to-coc-1906`, says Churches of Christ separated in 1906. The [Disciples' official history](https://disciples.org/our-identity/history-of-the-disciples/) describes gradual separation later recognized separately by the 1906 federal census. The same history supports the 1832 Stone-Campbell union and a separation beginning in 1926 with major withdrawals during 1967–69. Thus `disciples-to-independent-1926` has a defensible starting milestone, but its asserted 1926–1971 range was not fully verified in this sample.

Proposed review: label 1906 as census recognition; distinguish the 1832 movement union from later denominational restructuring, and represent the independent-church separation as a process. Preserve uncertainty about the exact endpoint until researched.

## P2 — Additional accuracy and neutrality review

### 9. Founding and naming conventions need qualifications, not automatic date replacement

* **Foursquare:** `data/sets/pentecostal-us/nodes.json` and `edges.json` give a 1923 founding and direct split from Assemblies of God. The [official timeline](https://www.foursquare.org/about/history/) supports Angelus Temple opening in 1923 and incorporation steps in 1927. It does not establish the direct denominational-split claim. Keep 1923 as a supported ministry milestone; independently research the parentage before deciding whether `split` is warranted.
* **Methodist Protestant:** `data/sets/methodist-us/{nodes,edges,events}.json` uses 1828. Official UMC material itself uses different milestones: [At-a-Glance](https://www.umc.org/en/content/united-methodists-at-a-glance) says a split in 1828, while its [historical series](https://www.umc.org/content/splits-separations-reconciliations-gcah-video-series) says denomination formation in 1830. [Emory's historical exhibition](https://dia.pitts.emory.edu/exhibits/exhibitcatalogs/Pitts_WesleyanaExhibitionCatalog_Spring2018.pdf) distinguishes the 1828 Associated Methodist Churches from the 1830 name. Treat this as milestone ambiguity, not a verified two-year error.
* **Catholic/Orthodox:** `catholic-church` in `presbyterian-europe/nodes.json` and `eastern-orthodox-tradition` in `baptist-europe/nodes.json` both start at 1054. That is a conventional schism marker, not a neutral institutional founding date. The [Orthodox Church in America's account](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/church-history/eleventh-century/the-great-schism) describes a separation that deepened over time. Add a milestone/date-basis explanation rather than choosing a new founding year on behalf of either tradition.
* **Anglican:** `anglican-us` uses 1785 as TEC formation. The official [Episcopal timeline](https://www.episcopalchurch.org/who-we-are/our-history/timeline-of-the-episcopal-church/) could not be fetched (403), so this audit does not settle 1785 versus later constitutional milestones. ACNA's description names Canadian participants but provides only a TEC parent edge; document the abstraction or add sourced contributors. The 1534 Church of England start similarly needs an explicit Reformation-milestone convention.
* **Adventist control check:** the 1863 SDA organization year in `adventist-us` agrees with the [official Adventist Yearbook](https://www.adventistyearbook.org/entity?EntityID=10010&highlight=oh) and [anniversary account](https://adventist.news/news/singing-prayer-mark-churchs-150th-anniversary-in-headquarters-ceremony). This verifies that date, not all Millerite parentage or membership claims.

### 10. Membership fields mix entity scope, observation dates, and definitions

Confirmed local problems:

* `data/sets/presbyterian-europe/nodes.json`, `swiss-reformed-zurich`, carries 1,782,513 members for the **Protestant Church in Switzerland**, not a Zurich-specific body. The parenthetical admits the different population, but assigning it to this node still makes a comparison misleading. `presbyterian-migrations-america` uses migrant totals as a size estimate rather than church membership.
* `data/sets/pentecostal-us/nodes.json`, `assemblies-of-god`, combines a 1914 US founding with 69–88.9 million members, without a year, a defined global federation versus US entity, or explanation of the range. The scope mismatch requires resolution; the range itself was not independently verified.
* `united-methodist-church`, `national-baptist-convention-of-america`, `foursquare-church`, `church-of-god-in-christ`, and `pentecostalism` have numeric estimates without observation years. “Members,” baptized members, adherents, confirmed members, and migrants are mixed across the graph. Historical 1986 membership also appears beside recent figures without a common comparison basis.
* `catholic-church` has 1.272 billion labeled 2025/WCD. The [Vatican's 2025 release](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/vatican-city/news/2025-03/pontifical-yearbook-2025-priests-religious-statistics.html) gives 1.406 billion baptized Catholics for **2023**, and its [2026 release](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/vatican-city/news/2026-03/new-data-from-annuario-pontificio-2026-shows-catholics-growing.html) gives just over 1.422 billion for **2024**. This is a verified discrepancy between estimates/definitions, not proof that the WCD number was mistranscribed. Research the WCD definition and reference year before replacing it.

Remedy: store value/range, observation year, geography, population definition, reporting body, and citation separately. Treat an undated website statistic as undated, even if accessed in 2026. Foursquare's current official history supports its approximate 8.8 million global figure but supplies no observation year; that does not make it a dated annual statistic.

### 11. Neutrality needs attribution and consistent standards

`data/sets/presbyterian-us/nodes.json`, `pca.summary`, adopts the denomination's mission language about faithfulness as narrator voice. Its [official about page](https://pcanet.org/about-the-pca/) supports this as self-description, not an independent assessment of faithfulness. The PCA edge's `causes` repeat these aspirations while its `reasons` make stronger allegations about the parent body. Make these fields consistent and attribute the founders' interpretation.

The OPC edge's `causes` includes “rejection of historic doctrines.” The [cited official history](https://www.opc.org/historian.html) verifies the 1936 founding, Machen's leadership, and 1939 name change, but presents a partisan theological evaluation of the parent church. Describe what the departing group alleged and supply contextual evidence rather than presenting the judgment as a settled denomination-wide fact. The OPC website's “nearly eight decades” membership wording is visibly undated historical copy, unsuitable as a current census.

Conversely, neutrality does not require obscuring slavery. `triennial-to-sbc-1845` has a defensible slavery basis: the [SBC's own 1995 resolution](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/resolution-on-racial-reconciliation-on-the-150th-anniversary-of-the-southern-baptist-convention/) acknowledges slavery's role in formation, and ABCUSA's history identifies the missionary slaveholder-appointment conflict. Add this more precise mechanism and direct evidence. PCUS's 1861 split and the 1958/1983 Presbyterian unions are supported at the broad date/participant level by the [Presbyterian Historical Society](https://pcusa.org/historical-society/history-online/history/history-church); this does not verify every motive, figure, or distinctive attached to those records.

## P2 — Coverage, consistency, and maintenance

The README explicitly prioritizes major American Protestant streams and excludes exhaustive global coverage. Missing global traditions are therefore a scope limitation, not automatically defects. Nevertheless, the current view needs an explicit coverage statement so a sparse graph is not mistaken for a complete family tree.

* **Disconnected coverage:** `eastern-orthodox-tradition` and WELS are entirely isolated. Six other nodes have no incoming lineage: Catholic Church, Second Great Awakening, ALC, LCA, EUB, and UPCNA. Some are intentional roots; the merged predecessor bodies show where ancestry research ends. The Orthodox record is stored in `baptist-europe` without a historical relationship, creating a misleading dataset grouping.
* **Major US omissions:** AME, AME Zion, CME, Congregational/UCC branches, Quakers, Anabaptist/Mennonite traditions, and several Reformed/Presbyterian branches are absent. This list is a proposed research backlog, not a finding that each belongs in the initial release. Within existing Methodist coverage, the Global Methodist Church is a particularly material recent omission: its [official launch document](https://globalmethodist.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/GM-Church-Who-We-Are.pdf) dates launch to May 1, 2022. Decide a cutoff before expanding.
* **Status inconsistency:** ten nodes are marked `dissolved` without an end year, including Millerism, English Separatists, Second Great Awakening, Methodism in England, and Presbyterian migrations. Some are diffuse movements for which “dissolved” may be inappropriate. Do not invent precise end dates merely to satisfy completeness; use a documented unknown/period convention.
* **Aggregate types:** German territorial churches and Scandinavian state churches are broad collections, not single denominations constituted in 1555/1536. Preserve them as explicitly labeled aggregates or add member bodies and milestone-specific dates. Similar distinctions apply to movements, polity, migrations, and legal denominations elsewhere.
* **Stale documentation:** `data/README.md` still presents US Presbyterian files as the primary dataset; the architecture model still lists old flat storage paths and an example `pcusa → pca` split in 1973, although its own PCUSA example begins in 1983. That example is historically and temporally wrong; the actual PCA parent is PCUS. Fix the example before it seeds new data. In `holiness-restorationist-us/notes/unknowns.md`, missing predecessor edges are still listed for Restoration Movement/Nazarene despite current edges, while “Missing end_year: None” conflicts with dissolved Second Great Awakening. Regenerate unknown inventories from JSON, with research questions maintained separately.

## Checks that passed and what they establish

All 44 discovered JSON files parsed successfully: 39 set entity files, two Presbyterian metadata files, and three common files. Required fields and integer-or-null year checks passed for the six collections. No duplicate IDs were found within those collections. No missing node endpoints, event node/edge references, or citation-to-source references were found. No directed cycles were detected. All citation records have nonempty locator/access-date values. These checks establish machine consistency only; they do not verify names, motives, chronology conventions, or source authority.

## Recommended remediation sequence

1. **Stabilize the integrated snapshot.** Finish the parent's source-bundle/validator work, then run read-only structural checks against raw files and bundle parity. Record counts and a revision identifier. Preserve the current uncommitted work and review historical edits separately.
2. **Close broken evidence references.** Resolve the six missing IDs with real evidence. Make unresolved citations fail validation, including references in key dates. Confirm all shared/set metadata reaches the explorer.
3. **Review high-impact chronology and lineage as one batch.** Prepare proposed changes for ELCA, ABCUSA/Triennial, Pentecostal foundations/adoption, Separatists, and Restoration census recognition, with before/after wording and sources. Have the user review historical claims before applying them.
4. **Agree on semantics.** Document influence versus institutional lineage, aggregate versus denomination, founding versus naming versus effective merger, year/range/approximation, and status conventions. Turn those rules into relationship-aware checks; influence-before-constitution should warn rather than automatically fail.
5. **Attach evidence at claim level.** Start with node dates, all parentage, causal assertions, and numeric statistics; then distinctive labels and key figures. Replace Wikipedia-only support with original/official records and independent historical scholarship where appropriate. Repair fragile locators and separately check inaccessible URLs.
6. **Normalize statistics and neutral language.** Separate global/US entities and statistic definitions, attribute confessional self-description, and document conflicting estimates. Mark unverified values visibly pending review rather than retaining false precision.
7. **Publish a bounded coverage plan.** State geographic/traditional scope and an as-of date; prioritize missing major branches within that scope. Repair isolated nodes/grouping and stale documentation. Refresh generated unknown inventories after approved data changes.

Completion criteria for a fully researched historical reference remain: zero unresolved references; documented date/relationship conventions; all high-impact dates and lineage claims supported by locatable evidence; no unqualified known chronological contradictions; statistics with population/year/geography; attributed disputed interpretations; and a visible, honest coverage statement. The original audit proposed changes only; subsequent user authorization permitted the enrichment and limited corrections documented below.

## Authorized dataset-wide enrichment follow-up

The user expanded the assignment from an audit into substantive enrichment of **every existing node**, including why it formed and what happened afterward, explained beliefs, people and roles, milestones, confessions and other key documents, contextual article links, and navigable successors. This follow-up implements that expanded scope across all 63 nodes rather than treating a sample as completion.

Every node now has `summary`, `formation_narrative`, explanatory `core_beliefs`, structured `key_figures`, `key_dates`, `key_documents`, `further_reading`, `ending_narrative`, `successor_ids`, `profile_scope`, and evidence in `citations`/`profile_citations`. People objects contain name, role, explanatory summary, article/source URL, and citations. Document objects distinguish adopted standards from inherited teaching, illustrative texts, primary records, and later explanations. Reading snippets are editorial descriptions, not purported quotations or automatic extracts from the linked page.

This is complete **profile coverage**, not a claim that every historical detail is now exhaustively researched. Broad movements and aggregates do not acquire fictitious founders, a universal creed, or an invented dissolution date. Where individual leadership or an original constitution remains insufficiently researched, the text says so and provides the established context instead. Inherited membership estimates, some old notes, and Wikipedia-based citations remain separate audit liabilities; new profile citations do not retroactively verify those older fields.

### Changes and evidence decisions

* The six previously unresolved citation IDs now have researched entries with identified sources and locators. The Separatist relationship citation explicitly does **not** validate a split in 1534; that edge's precise year is now unknown. Holiness origins and the Genevan exile congregation are supported by denominational historical material and the Geneva State Archives respectively.
* ELCA operational start and the three institutional merger edges/event now use 1988, while 1987 remains a separate constituting-convention milestone. Existing IDs ending in `1987` are deliberately retained for reference stability. Predecessor `end_year: 1987` still denotes their last full pre-union calendar year and needs an eventual schema-wide date convention.
* The Triennial-to-ABCUSA edge now describes missionary heritage and coordination rather than a direct 1907 renaming. Additional research in the [official successor agency's history](https://internationalministries.org/history/) establishes the American Baptist Missionary Union name in 1846, followed by Foreign Mission Society in 1910 and Board of International Ministries in 1973. The original convention-stage `end_year` is now 1846. The immediate missionary successor is absent from the graph and is proposed below, not falsely equated with ABCUSA.
* Earlier Church of God foundations are distinguished from later Pentecostal influence. The Cleveland influence edge is undated; COGIC's Pentecostal development is dated 1907. The inherited exact National Baptist parentage of COGIC remains explicitly low-confidence with unknown split year.
* The Foursquare direct-split assertion remains an explicitly low-confidence research hypothesis. The verified 1923 ministry beginning and 1927 incorporation steps are explained separately. This is not described as a proven direct denominational split.
* Churches of Christ's 1906 marker is explained as census recognition of a developing separation. The inherited 1926–1971 independent-church range remains a research limit rather than a newly verified exact span.
* English Separatists no longer carry a blanket believer's-baptism distinctive. The PCUSA node no longer carries an unsupported denomination-wide `Barthianist` label. Its constitutional confessions receive a fuller explanation.
* Morton **H.** Smith is explained as the Reformed theologian and PCA founding stated clerk, distinct from Morton Smith the ancient historian. Named entries now include short context and evidence rather than names alone.
* The Church of Scotland's new Book of Confessions is identified as effective January 2026. The rejected proposed 2026 Advent Christian declaration is not presented as adopted doctrine. These checks illustrate why a linked document's institutional status matters as much as its title.
* All 70 relationships now carry a narrative, causes, people, milestone representation, and explanatory reading/context references. Added formation context concerns the target node; it is **not** independent corroboration of every original edge assertion. Existing detailed Presbyterian narratives were preserved. The three longstanding Scottish influence edges still legitimately require different temporal rules from institutional-origin edges.

### Evidence and remaining research boundaries

The new corpus includes official doctrinal statements, historic confessions and catechisms, archival catalogs and records, institutional histories, scholarly research, and contemporary event reporting. Some historical texts are hosted in confessional transcription collections; those collections' editorial interpretation is not treated as the same thing as the primary text. Official accounts of departure or doctrinal fidelity remain attributed viewpoints. The profiles preserve differences among General/Particular Baptists, pre-Baptist Separatists, Holiness and Pentecostal teaching, and constitution versus effective merger.

Source records are not necessarily unique works: some new profile sources duplicate URLs already present under older IDs. This preserves existing consumers and should be followed by a reviewed source-alias/deduplication pass. Counts below are record counts, not counts of independent corroborating witnesses.

The following remain priorities for deeper work: original NBCA and PNBC constitutions; exact adoption/subscription wording of older ALC/LCA and UPCNA standards; full national and territorial histories behind regional Lutheran aggregates; original proceedings behind the now-supported Triennial/Missionary Union transition; fuller reasons and competing-party accounts for Presbyterian and Lutheran conflicts; current statistics with consistent definitions; and original-text editions where only an institutional catalog or retrospective account has been linked.
