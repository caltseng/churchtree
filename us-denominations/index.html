Comparison notes

Published detail covers selected reported affiliations, not an exhaustive inventory. No residual is inferred. Totals and children may differ because of rounding or available detail.

Published estimates are independently rounded and need not add to the parent or to 100%.

Affiliation values are shares of U.S. adults, not membership rolls or children. Upper thresholds are not zero estimates. Each Source link gives the publisher, date, exact table locator, column and caveats; the row is identified beside the estimate. Methods, classification, precision and reuse.

Demographic definitions

Only Christian, other religions and religiously unaffiliated aggregates are selected. Nonresponses remain in each subgroup denominator but are not plotted; the published nonresponse note is retained. Independently rounded estimates need not sum to 100%. No residual, sum, re-rounding or denominator reversal is used. No intersectional combinations, denomination estimates or attendance answers are inferred. Other demographic populations and religious details are outside this excerpt.

How the charts work

Families and denominations and Protestant traditions use a drill-down pie. Its full circle is the selected group's published share of U.S. adults (all U.S. adults at the top); each slice is one listed affiliation's published share of all U.S. adults, drawn to that scale. Select a slice, or focus the pie and use arrow keys and Enter, to zoom into its published children or, for a leaf, its sourced details. The centre, Back, Reset and breadcrumbs zoom out. When independently rounded children total more than their parent, the circle is scaled to their total and says so. A hatched arc marks the part of a parent not itemized in selected published detail; it is not an estimate. Leaves have no finer published breakdown, so no further pie is drawn. Each level shows a compact source credit with direct links; coverage, record notes, locators, precision and the reuse disclaimer are under Info. Animation is skipped when reduced motion is requested. The ranked list below each pie keeps the exact values, navigation and source notes; List mode hides the pie. Back restores open source notes and Info during this visit.

Attendance and demographic comparisons keep zero-based bars on a fixed scale. Pinch inside those charts to zoom, then drag to pan; zoom buttons and keyboard controls provide non-gesture alternatives without changing estimates or denominators. Browser page zoom remains available outside the chart.

Only numeric estimates have slices or bars. Published upper thresholds retain their exact strings; unavailable or suppressed estimates have reasons and no slice or bar. Selected detail never implies complete coverage; no residuals, sums of censored cells or inferred denominations are added.

Classification and precision

Pew's mainline, evangelical and historically Black Protestant traditions use denomination and, for some nonspecific responses, race and/or born-again identification. Pew reports that 36% of Protestants were classified using the latter approach. Mainline is a survey classification; some rows are families, networks or general identities, not single denominations. Appendix B: Classification of Protestant denominations. Nondenominational and nonspecific Protestant responses remain separate.

The report's tradition decimals (23.1%, 11.5%, 5.4%) and interactive displays (23%, 11%, 5%) are separate published presentations. Report decimals take precedence for the matched wave, denominator and taxonomy. Ordinary rounding does not explain 11.5% versus 11%; no unrounded microdata was used to explain it.
