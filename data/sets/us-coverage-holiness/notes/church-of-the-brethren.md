# Church of the Brethren

Reviewed November 22, 2026. Audit rank 45, census code 157. The 1723 marker means the first American congregation at Germantown, Pennsylvania. The 1708 Schwarzenau baptisms began the European movement, and 1908 marks the present U.S. denominational name. The profile follows the U.S. continuing branch through selected developments and a documented 2020 departure.

- **Covered within stated scope** — The [denominational history](https://www.brethren.org/about/history/) and [Brethren Historical Library and Archives sketches](https://www.brethren.org/bhla/sketches/) document the 1708 baptisms, Alexander Mack and Johanna Kipping, Peter Becker's leadership of migration, and the 1723 congregation. The member who baptized Mack is unnamed in these sources. The [polity manual](https://www.brethren.org/ac/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2023/02/ch.-0-MOP-Overview-Feb.-2023.pdf), p. 1 and note 1, reproduces an excerpt from a 1708 letter and identifies its published transcription.
- **Covered within stated scope** — The [archival guide](https://www.brethren.org/bhla/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2019/07/guide-to-reseach-in-brethren-history.pdf) identifies the historic German Baptist Brethren and colloquial Dunker/Dunkard names and the 1908 name adoption. The continuing body's archival sketches and [the Progressive branch's account](https://brethrenchurch.org/history-series-this-chapter-in-brethren-hymnology-centers-on-henry-holsinger/) support the account of disagreement and Holsinger's role. The Old Order departure is described at an introductory level. The exact sequence and claims across its conferences need review against its own records and early Annual Meeting minutes. The [1948 service program history](https://www.brethren.org/bvs/history/) documents youth participation and institutional development.
- **Covered within stated scope** — The published [beliefs](https://www.brethren.org/about/beliefs/), [practices](https://www.brethren.org/about/practices/) and [1991 Annual Conference peacemaking statement](https://www.brethren.org/ac/statements/1991-peacemaking/) document present identity and the links between peace, conscientious objection and service. The profile attributes the recent separation to participants' statements. [Covenant Brethren](https://covenantbrethren.org/frequently-asked-questions/) describes its doctrinal commitments and 2020 incorporation. [Church of the Brethren leaders](https://www.brethren.org/news/2020/leadership-team-issues-update-in-response-to-activities-of-the-covenant-brethren-church/) record their objection to recruitment. The Church of the Brethren continues.
- **Missing records and tradition balance** — No existing node represents the early Schwarzenau movement or an immediate partner or daughter body. Priority A is any future separate movement node (1708 baptisms) and a historically qualified link to the American continuation. Priority B includes the Old Order German Baptist Brethren, Progressive Brethren Church (1883 organizing), and Covenant Brethren Church (2020 incorporation and separate denominational meeting), whose departure links would need their own reviewed records and date semantics. The profile includes Kipping and the youth initiative. Additional research is needed on Black Brethren, overseas mission, and twentieth-century disputes. Plymouth Brethren and Brethren in Christ have distinct histories.
- **Scale** — [2020 U.S. Religion Census group-detail workbook](https://www.usreligioncensus.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/2020_USRC_Group_Detail.xlsx), national row 157, reports 109,288 adherents and 921 congregations. The figures describe 2020 and do not estimate later affiliation changes.
