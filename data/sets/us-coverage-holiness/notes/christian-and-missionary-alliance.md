# Christian and Missionary Alliance

Reviewed November 22, 2026. Audit rank 23, census code 089. The timeline marker is the U.S. denomination's 1974 declaration and restructuring. The account reaches back through its missionary-society predecessors and includes selected developments through the 2025 Council.

- **Covered within stated scope** — The [U.S. Alliance history](https://cmalliance.org/who-we-are/our-history/) explains the Gospel Tabernacle, Missionary Training Institute, the early workforce (including the role of women), Paul Rader's succession and the later shift to denominational organization. The [1887 constitutions](https://online.ambrose.edu/alliancestudies/ahtreadings/ahtr_s4.html) and [Board of Managers finding aid](https://ambrose.edu/sites/default/files/2000-02_cma_board_of_managers_0.pdf) identify the Christian Alliance and the Evangelical Missionary Alliance (later the International Missionary Alliance) as the two society partners.
- **Unsupported/conflicted** — The [1916 federal religious-bodies report](https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/1919/demographics/religious-bodies-1916/1916-religious-bodies-part-2.pdf) dates the society union to 1895. The institutional archival finding aid dates it to 1897, which is the year used in the profile with attribution. Confirm the chronology against the original merger proceedings and charter and investigate whether 1895 marks an earlier step. The 1974 marker follows the Alliance's own history and refers to its denominational declaration. The sequence of earlier decisions and effective constitutional changes needs Council minutes.
- **Covered within stated scope** — [Simpson's 1890 revised third edition](https://archive.org/details/fourfoldgospel00simp) explains the formative Fourfold Gospel. The [published Alliance confession and interpretive statements](https://cmalliance.org/who-we-are/our-faith-and-mission/perspectives/) describe present teaching. The [2025 Council report](https://cdn.cmalliance.org/wordpress/cmalliance/Council-2025-Report-and-Minutes.pdf) documents ongoing U.S. governance. The [Alliance timeline](https://a101.cmalliance.org/downloads/theC%26MA_Timeline_2019-01.pdf) dates Canadian autonomy to 1981.
- **Missing records, priority A** — The immediate 1974 predecessor is the united C&MA missionary society (1897 union, archival date). Its own predecessors, the Christian Alliance and International Missionary Alliance (1887 organizing), would be the immediate parents of that society in a graph expansion. These are narrated in the profile. Neither Simpson's Presbyterian background nor the existing Holiness context node establishes institutional parentage.
- **Missing research, priority B** — Test claims of early Pentecostal ruptures and later doctrinal disagreements against Council minutes and accounts from the participants. Research the leadership and sending roles of individual women missionaries through mission records. The histories of the Alliance World Fellowship and autonomous churches outside the U.S. are beyond this account. The U.S. body continues after Canadian autonomy.
- **Scale** — [2020 U.S. Religion Census group-detail workbook](https://www.usreligioncensus.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/2020_USRC_Group_Detail.xlsx), national row 089, reports 414,360 adherents and 1,776 congregations. These are U.S. census measures for 2020.
