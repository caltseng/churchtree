# Christian Methodist Episcopal Church

Audit rank 31, census group code `101`. Reviewed November 22, 2026. Covers Reconstruction-era organization, selected twentieth-century institutional and civil-rights developments, and the 2020 U.S. adherent count. It is an introductory U.S. profile of a wider church.

Covered within stated scope: initiative of Black Methodists, Southern conference authorization, 1870 organization and founding episcopacy, later governance, civil-rights participation and 1954 name resolution. Unsupported/conflicted: denominational accounts describe founding attendance at different levels (participants, delegates, congregations); no delegate total is asserted here. Missing research (B): compare the original 1866 and 1870 proceedings and recent editions of the CME Discipline for changes in constitutional practice. Later regional disputes and overseas expansion are out of scope.

- [About the Christian Methodist Episcopal Church](https://thecmechurch.org/about-us/), Connectional Council paragraph. Authorized by General Conference in 1946 and operational in 1950.
- [Christian Methodist Episcopal Church](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/christian-methodist-episcopal-church), 1954 General Conference paragraph. General Conference approved the new name in Memphis in 1954.

- [About the Christian Methodist Episcopal Church](https://thecmechurch.org/about-us/), Connectional Council and church departments. Continuing connectional denomination and its organizational departments.

- [History of Lane College](https://lanecollege.edu/about/history-of-lane-college), first session of CME High School. Jennie E. Lane led the school at its opening in 1882.
