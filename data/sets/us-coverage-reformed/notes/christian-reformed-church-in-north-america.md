# Christian Reformed Church in North America

Audit rank 41, census code 105. The 1857 marker is the seceding congregations' organization. The Dutch Seceders were antecedents of some migrants and the Classis of Holland belonged to the body now called the RCA.

**Covered within scope.** Compare the [joint history](https://rca.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/Synod2014-ConciseHistory-RCA-CRC.pdf) and [early assembly minutes](https://library.calvin.edu/hh/acts-of-synod). The profile attributes complaints to the 1857 participants and distinguishes later disputes over Freemasonry. The CRC and [Hoeksema's testimony](https://www.prca.org/resources/pamphlets/why-protestant-reformed-an-early-testimony-to-the-crc) give perspectives on the 1924–1925 separation. The CRC's ordination history and [URCNA history](https://www.urcna.org/about) document distinct perspectives on a later division. The 2017 Synod placed Belhar in a different category from the historic confessions.

**Priority A missing merger partner.** The eastern True Protestant Dutch Reformed Church is named in the account of the 1890 union. Use early assembly records to distinguish its earlier separation, historical names, and constituent congregations. Sources vary the word order of the English name. The profile follows the CRC chronology.

**Priority B missing relationships and episodes.** Protestant Reformed Churches in America and United Reformed Churches in North America are distinct potential records. Research early assemblies and departing parties' reasons before modeling them. The profile samples U.S. and Canadian history through 2017. Later human-sexuality disputes, women's agency and Indigenous mission histories need additional work.
